# Learning techniques that work

## Interleaving

Mixing problem types within one study session, instead of practising one type at a time, makes
students better at choosing the right method for a new problem. It feels slower and more
confusing while studying, but test scores a week later are higher.

## The testing effect

A short quiz right after studying produces better long-term retention than spending the same
minutes rereading. In the classic experiments (Roediger and Karpicke, 2006) students who took one
recall test remembered about 50% more after a week than students who restudied the text.

## Spacing and the forgetting curve

Spaced repetition works because forgetting is steepest just after learning. New fact: the best
gap before the next review is roughly 10–20% of the time you want to remember the material, so
for an exam in 30 days the first review belongs about 3–6 days after studying.
